# Cooking + calling home

Decided after the ride that I'd actually cook something real tonight instead of the usual fifteen-minute rescue-mission dinner. Picked a braise — the kind of thing that punishes you for starting late, so I got going around 4:30 to have any chance of eating before 8.

## What I made

Braised short ribs, the long slow way — seared first, then into the pot with onion, carrot, celery, a splash of red wine, stock, a couple of bay leaves, and way more garlic than any recipe would tell you to use. Let it go low in the oven for about three hours while I did approximately nothing productive, which was the entire appeal.

Made a side of mashed potatoes near the end, and a very simple green salad just to pretend the meal had some balance to it. The whole kitchen smelled like red wine and onions for hours, which I don't mind at all.

Notes for next time:
- Should have seared the ribs in two batches instead of crowding the pot — didn't get as much color as I wanted
- Three hours was almost exactly right, maybe another 15 minutes would have made the meat fall apart even more
- Forgot to buy fresh thyme, used dried, was fine but not as good
- The good knife needs sharpening, noticed it fighting through the carrots

Ate around 7:45, later than planned but not by much. Worth every bit of the wait. Leftovers for at least two more dinners this week, which solves a problem I hadn't even started worrying about yet — one less thing to think about on a week that's already shaping up to be full.

Texted Dax a photo of the pot mid-braise because he'd asked what I was up to. He replied with something about how he'd ordered takeout for the third night running, no shame about it at all, which is fair, not everyone needs a three-hour dinner project on a Sunday.

## Call home

Called around 6, while the braise was still doing its thing in the oven, so I'd have an excuse to just sit and talk without also doing something else at the same time — a rare state for me. Should have known better than to expect that to go smoothly.

First ten minutes was fine, then Mom brought up Tuesday — I'd said I'd call back that evening and then didn't, got pulled into a Windpipe incident review that ran long and just... forgot. She wasn't loud about it, which is somehow worse, just that flat "I figured you were busy" that means the opposite of what it says. I apologized, meant it, and it still didn't really land.

Then the visit thing came up again. Rhea's apparently already booked her flights for next month and Mom wants to know when I'm coming, and I don't actually know yet because I have no idea what October looks like at work — we haven't even scoped the next quarter's on-call rotation — and saying that out loud made it worse, not better. "You always say that" is not wrong, is the annoying part. I've said some version of it for three visits running now.

Didn't turn into a real fight. It never does, we're not a family that fights loudly, it just goes quiet and a little clipped and then someone changes the subject. Talked about Rhea's garden for a bit as the reset button. Asked about sleep, gave my usual not-quite-honest answer. Hung up after about forty minutes instead of the hour I was expecting, and I don't think that's a coincidence.

No neat ending here. I know the actual fix is just picking dates and committing even when the quarter's uncertain, and I know I'm not going to do that tonight. Going to sit with the slightly bad feeling instead of trying to write my way out of it. The short ribs helped more than the phone call did, which feels like it says something.

Part of what stings is that I know the guilt is somewhat manufactured on my end — nobody actually demanded an apology tour, I just supply one automatically now whenever the visit topic comes up. Might be worth actually sitting down this week and looking at the calendar for real instead of leaving it as a vague future problem. Not tonight though. Tonight is short ribs and a book.
